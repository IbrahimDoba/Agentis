AI agents help fintechs convert more WhatsApp leads by fixing the points where interested people drop off: slow first replies, half-answered questions, missing details, and no follow-up after the customer goes quiet. The agent does not make your product more attractive. It stops you losing people who already wanted it.

Think of the journey from "I saw your ad" to "I have completed sign-up" as a pipe with five leaks. Here is each one and what an agent does about it.

### Leak 1: the reply comes hours later

A person who taps a click-to-WhatsApp ad for a business loan or a POS terminal is interested right now. If your first reply arrives the next morning, you are talking to someone who has already messaged a competitor.

An AI agent replies in seconds, at any hour, on the number the ad points to. On Dailzero it also knows which Facebook or Instagram ad the person came from, so a customer who clicked the "POS for shop owners" ad is not asked "how may we help you today?"

### Leak 2: the first answer does not answer

Many support replies to a lead look like "Please visit our website for details" or "Kindly download the app". That is a dead end. The lead asked a question because they wanted the answer in the chat.

> Hello, how much is your POS machine and is there any daily target?

A good reply states the terminal price or caution fee you set, explains your transaction target and what happens if it is missed, gives the settlement time, and then asks one question back: "What kind of business do you run, and where is it?" Price, terms, next step, all in one message.

### Leak 3: nobody collects the details

A lead your sales team can act on has a name, a location, a business type and a clear need. Most WhatsApp chats end before anyone asks. The agent asks as part of the conversation, one question at a time, and keeps what the customer says.

For a lender that might be the amount, the purpose, and whether the applicant is salaried or self-employed. For a merchant product it might be the shop location and rough daily sales.

### Leak 4: the handover to a person is clumsy

At some point a human has to take over: to approve, to visit the shop, to call. Conversion dies here when the officer opens the chat and asks everything again.

With an agent, people who show buying intent are listed as leads in the dashboard on every plan. Your officer sees the chat history and the details already collected, replies from the dashboard or their own phone, and the AI pauses on that chat. The customer experiences one continuous conversation.

> Okay I'm interested. Can somebody call me tomorrow morning?

Good reply: confirm the number to call and the best time, tell the customer an officer will be in touch, and hand over. Do not let the agent promise a specific time your team has not agreed to keep.

### Leak 5: the lead goes quiet and nobody checks back

Plenty of people ask, get the answer, say "okay let me think about it" and vanish. Most teams never message them again because nobody remembers to.

On the Pro plan, Dailzero sends automated follow-ups to customers who went quiet. One polite nudge a day or two later, answering the likely objection, recovers some of these. Keep it to one or two. More than that reads as pestering and can get your number reported.

### What the agent cannot do for conversion

- **It cannot approve a loan or open an account.** Credit decisions and KYC stay in your systems and with your staff.
- **It cannot check an application's status** unless a developer connects your backend through webhook tools. Without that, "what is the status of my application?" goes to a person.
- **It cannot fix an uncompetitive offer.** If leads drop off the moment they hear your rate, the leak is the rate.

### A simple way to see which leak is yours

Take 30 leads from last month that did not convert and read each chat to the end. Note where it stopped: no reply from you, a vague reply, no details taken, stalled at handover, or silence from the customer after a good answer. The pile with the most chats is where to start.
