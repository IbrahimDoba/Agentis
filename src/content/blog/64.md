You automate product recommendations on WhatsApp by giving an AI agent your product list with a short "who this is for" note on each item, plus the one or two questions it should ask before suggesting anything. The agent matches each customer's answer to your list and replies with a pick, a price and a photo.

Think of it as a well-trained shop attendant who knows your shelf. Here is the setup.

### Step 1: describe every product by what it is for

A name and a price are not enough to recommend from. Compare:

- "Vitamin C serum, ₦8,500"
- "Vitamin C serum, ₦8,500. For dark spots and uneven tone. Suits oily and combination skin. Not for very sensitive skin."

The second version lets the agent match a need to a product. Do this for every item. This step decides how good the recommendations are.

### Step 2: write the questions the agent should ask

A good attendant asks before suggesting. Give the agent the questions for your category, and keep it to two at most.

- **Skincare:** skin type, and the main concern.
- **Gifts:** who it is for, and the budget.
- **Wigs and hair:** preferred length, and budget.
- **Phones and gadgets:** what they mainly use it for, and budget.

If the customer has already answered in their first message, the agent should not ask again.

### Step 3: upload photos to the media library

A recommendation without a picture rarely closes. Upload a clear photo for each product so the agent can send it with the suggestion. An image reply costs 8 credits against about 5 for text, so instruct the agent to send one photo of its top pick, not the whole catalogue.

### Step 4: set the rules

Write these as plain instructions:

1. Suggest at most two options, and say which one you would pick and why.
2. Stay inside the customer's stated budget. If nothing fits, say so and name the closest item.
3. Never claim a product treats a medical condition. For rashes, reactions or anything that sounds like a health problem, hand the chat to a person.

### Step 5: test with messages the way customers really write

Before going live, send the agent the kind of messages you actually receive. For example:

> "Abeg which cream good for black spots, my budget na 10k"

A good reply looks like this:

> "For dark spots within ₦10,000, I would go with the Vitamin C serum at ₦8,500. It suits oily and combination skin. Is your skin oily, dry or sensitive? I want to be sure it is right for you before you order."

The agent copes with Pidgin and everyday Nigerian English in customer messages and replies in English. Also test two other things customers do:

- **A photo.** Customers often send a screenshot and ask for "something like this". The agent understands photos customers send and can point to the closest item on your list, or say you do not stock it.
- **A voice note.** It understands those too.

### Step 6: go live, then read the chats

In the first two weeks, read through the conversations every few days. Look for questions the agent could not answer and products it never suggests. Both usually mean a vague product note.

### A second example: gift recommendations

Gift shops get the vaguest messages, especially around Valentine's Day and December:

> "I need a birthday gift for my wife, around 25k. Delivery to Port Harcourt."

With "who this is for" notes on each item, the agent can answer with one clear pick:

> "For ₦25,000, the jewellery and perfume gift box at ₦22,000 is our most popular birthday choice for wives. Delivery to Port Harcourt is ₦3,000 and takes 2 to 3 days. Should I send you a photo?"

### What these recommendations cannot do

- **They do not know your stock.** The agent works from the list you typed in. Remove or mark items that are sold out. Checking live stock needs a developer to connect your system through webhook tools.
- **They are not based on browsing behaviour.** Nothing tracks what a customer viewed on a website. The agent does remember returning customers and earlier chats.
- **They do not complete payment.** After the customer chooses, the agent collects order details, shares the payment details you gave it and hands the chat to you to confirm the transfer.

### Frequently asked questions

#### How many products can the agent recommend from?

What matters is the description, not the count. A store with 30 well-described items will get better recommendations than one with 300 bare names.

#### Do I need a website or an online catalogue?

No. The agent works from the products, prices and photos you add during setup. A website is optional, though Dailzero also offers a chat widget if you have one.

#### Can I try it before paying?

Yes. The free trial lasts 7 days and includes 1,000 credits, about 200 AI messages, which is enough to test recommendations on real customers.
