Whether WhatsApp automation saves you 10 hours a week depends on how many hours you spend replying now, and most owners have never counted. Your phone already has the figure. Check it, work out what share is repeat questions, and you will know your own number in about 15 minutes.

Nobody can honestly promise you 10 hours without that count.

### Step 1: find your weekly WhatsApp hours

Your phone records how long each app is open.

- **iPhone:** Settings, then Screen Time, then See All App & Website Activity. Switch to Week and find WhatsApp Business.
- **Android:** Settings, then Digital Wellbeing and parental controls. Tap the chart and find WhatsApp Business.

Write down the total for last week. If you use one WhatsApp for both business and family, take the total and make an honest estimate of the business share.

This figure is an undercount. It misses the time spent in your bank app checking an alert or in your gallery hunting for a product photo.

### Step 2: work out how much of it is repeat work

Open your business chats and go through the last 50 conversations. Mark each one as either:

- **Repeat:** a question you have answered many times, with one correct answer. Price, availability, location, delivery fee, account number, opening time.
- **Judgment:** a complaint, a negotiation, a custom request, a confirmation only you can make.

Count the repeats and divide by 50. That is your repeat share.

### Step 3: do the sum

Weekly WhatsApp hours × repeat share = hours an AI agent could take over.

**A worked example.** Say Emeka sells phone accessories in Computer Village, Ikeja. His Screen Time shows 16 hours on WhatsApp Business last week. Of his last 50 chats, 36 were repeat questions, which is 72%.

16 hours × 0.72 = 11.5 hours a week.

**A smaller example.** A tailor in Enugu spends 6 hours a week on WhatsApp, and only 20 of her last 50 chats were repeats, because most customers are discussing styles and measurements. That is 40%.

6 hours × 0.40 = 2.4 hours a week.

Both figures are made up to show the method. The first owner clears the 10-hour mark. The second does not come close, and for her the value is elsewhere: new customers answered at night while she sleeps.

### Where the hours actually go

Look closely at the repeat pile and the same tasks keep appearing. One answer takes seconds. The trouble is giving it forty times a day.

- Typing prices that are already on your price list.
- Finding and resending the same product photos.
- Sending your account details and delivery fees, again.
- Asking for name, address and phone number one message at a time.
- Replying "yes, still available".

A typical exchange looks like this:

> How much is the 20000mah power bank and do you deliver to Ajah?

> The 20,000mAh power bank is ₦18,500. Delivery to Ajah is ₦3,000 by dispatch rider, usually same day if you order before 2pm. Would you like one or more?

That price is invented for the example. With Dailzero, the agent sends replies like this in seconds from the products and prices you typed in, sends the photo from your media library, collects the delivery details and shares the payment information you gave it.

### What stays on your plate

Be realistic when you plan your week. These still take your time:

- **Confirming payment.** Dailzero does not process or verify payments. You still check the alert.
- **Stock.** The agent knows what you told it. If the last unit sells, update it.
- **Handed-over chats.** Complaints, bargaining and special requests come to you.
- **Setup and upkeep.** Expect a few hours in the first week to add products, prices and FAQs, then a few minutes a day reading chats and filling gaps.

So subtract a little from your Step 3 figure.
