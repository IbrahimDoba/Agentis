To track customer inquiries on WhatsApp, record three things for every chat: what the customer wants, what stage they have reached, and who owns the next reply. You can do this by hand with WhatsApp Business labels, or let an AI agent answer every chat and list the likely buyers in a dashboard.

### Why inquiries get lost in the first place

WhatsApp sorts chats by the latest message, not by importance. A customer who asked for your account number at 9am is pushed down the screen by twenty "how much?" messages before lunch.

For a store, the usual leaks are:

- A customer got the price, went quiet, and nobody followed up.
- Order details were collected, but payment was never confirmed.
- Someone paid, and the order was not passed to dispatch.
- A complaint was read by one person and assumed handled by another.

### Give every chat one of five stages

Keep the list short enough to remember:

1. **New.** Asked a question, not yet answered.
2. **Interested.** Got an answer and is still talking, or said they will get back to you.
3. **Order taken.** You have the item, quantity, name and address.
4. **Awaiting payment.** Account details sent, no alert yet.
5. **Paid, to dispatch.** Confirmed, waiting for the rider.

Add a sixth, **Problem**, for complaints and returns. Every chat should sit in exactly one stage.

### Method one: by hand, with labels

The WhatsApp Business app lets you attach coloured labels to chats. Create one label per stage and move each chat along as it progresses. It is free, and it works for a store with a handful of chats a day.

It breaks down as volume rises, because someone must remember to change every label.

### Method two: let an AI agent do the first three stages

With Dailzero linked to your number, the tracking that depends on speed and memory happens without you:

- **Nothing stays "New".** The agent replies within seconds, day and night, from the prices and policies you gave it.
- **Buyers are separated from browsers.** People showing buying intent are listed as leads in the dashboard, on every plan.
- **Order details are collected.** Name, address, size and quantity are gathered in the chat.
- **Returning customers are recognised.** The agent remembers earlier conversations, so a repeat buyer does not start from zero.
- **Ownership is clear.** When the customer asks for a person, or the agent cannot help, the chat is handed over. Each team member can have their own login, and when one of you replies the AI pauses on that chat until you switch it back on.
- **You see the source.** If the customer came from a Facebook or Instagram click-to-WhatsApp ad, the dashboard shows which one.
- **Quiet customers get a nudge.** On the Pro plan, automated follow-ups go to people who stopped replying.

Here is where the handover matters. A kitchenware store gets:

> "Good morning, I paid for the non-stick pot set on Friday. Has it gone out?"

The agent cannot see your bank or the rider's progress, so the right reply is honest:

> "Good morning. Let me get a member of the team to check your payment and dispatch status. They will reply here shortly."

### What you still have to track yourself

Dailzero does not process or verify payments and cannot see delivery status on its own. So stages four and five, awaiting payment and paid, stay with you. A simple paid-orders book or spreadsheet that you update by hand is enough for most stores.

There is also no built-in sync with Google Sheets, CRMs, Shopify or WooCommerce. If you want orders written straight into your own system, a developer can connect it using webhook tools, described on the [developers page](/developers).

### A ten-minute daily routine

**Morning.** Open the leads list. Message anyone from yesterday who showed interest but did not order. Clear every handed-over chat.

**Afternoon.** Match bank alerts against orders taken. Mark the paid ones and give them to dispatch.

**Evening.** Update the agent's product list with anything that sold out or changed price. Check the Problem chats and make sure each has an owner.

### Which method fits your store

If you get fewer than ten chats a day and you answer them all yourself, labels are enough. If messages arrive while you sleep, more than one person replies, or you run ads, the manual method will start leaking. ### Frequently asked questions

#### Can I track WhatsApp inquiries without any paid tool?

Yes. Use labels in the WhatsApp Business app, one per stage, and a notebook or spreadsheet for paid orders. It works at low volume but depends on someone updating every chat by hand.

#### Does Dailzero show me which customers are ready to buy?

It lists people who show buying intent as leads in the dashboard, on every plan.

#### Will it tell a customer where their order is?

Not on its own. It cannot see delivery status, so it hands those chats to you unless a developer has connected your order system through webhook tools.
